How Grading Affects a Card's Value

The same card can be worth $100 raw and $2,000 in a PSA 10 holder. Understanding that ladder is half of understanding card prices.

The tiers

Professional graders (PSA, BGS, CGC, SGC) assign a condition grade from 1 to 10. Market prices cluster into tiers: raw (ungraded), Grade 7, 8, 9, 9.5, and 10. Each step up is worth more — but not linearly. The jump from 9 to 10 is usually the largest by far, because supply thins out fastest at the top.

Reading a grading ladder

The ratio between a card's PSA 10 price and its raw price tells you how hard the card is to grade well and how much collectors care. A modern card fresh from a pack might carry a 2–4× ladder. A vintage card with fragile edges can run 20–40×, because gem copies barely exist. When a ladder looks impossible — a $10 raw price under a $1,000 PSA 10 — one of the two numbers is usually wrong, and on CardStock that pattern triggers a data review rather than a forecast.

When grading is worth it

Grading costs money and months. The break-even question: does (graded price − raw price) exceed the grading fee, times your honest probability of the top grade? Centering, print lines, and surface issues you can barely see will cap most cards at a 9 — and for many cards the 9-vs-raw premium is small. The cards that reward grading are the ones where you have real evidence of gem condition and the 10-ladder is steep.
