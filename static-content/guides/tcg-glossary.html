Trading Card Market Glossary

The vocabulary of card markets, in plain English.

Condition & grading

NM (Near Mint): the standard "good copy" condition for raw cards; most quoted raw prices assume it. Raw: ungraded. Slab: a graded card in its sealed holder. Gem: a perfect-grade copy (PSA 10 / BGS 9.5+). Grading ladder: the price staircase from raw up through the graded tiers. Pop (population): how many copies a grader has certified at each grade — low pop at high grade is where records happen.

Prices

Market price: a venue's estimate of what the card sells for, usually modeled from recent sales. Ask / listing price: what a seller wants; not a price until someone pays it. Spread: the gap between what buyers pay and sellers ask. Comp: a recent sale of the same (or similar) card used as evidence of value. Frozen price: a displayed value that hasn't changed in weeks — usually a sign there are no sales behind it.

Market behavior

Liquidity: how quickly a card sells near its quoted price. High-liquidity cards have tight, trustworthy prices; illiquid ones can be days of waiting or big discounts. Chase card: the set's most-wanted pulls, which carry most of a set's value. Launch decay: the typical price slide in a set's first weeks as supply floods in — most cards open high and settle. Spike: a sudden run-up, often tournament- or social-media-driven; spikes retrace more often than they hold.

Printings

Printing / variant: the same card in different physical forms — 1st Edition vs Unlimited, holo vs reverse holo, foil stamps, alternate arts. Variants are different markets with different prices; a chart that mixes them is measuring soup. Serialized: numbered print runs (e.g. /99); ultra-scarce and priced accordingly.
