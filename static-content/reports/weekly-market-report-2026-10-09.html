Weekly Market Report — October 9, 2026

Published 2026-10-09 · CardStock weekly market report

Gundam was the week's weakest market, falling 3.3 percent as its two newest releases, Starter Deck 14: Heavy Dominion and Eternal Nexus, saw the bulk of their tracked cards slide. Pokémon moved the other way, up 0.7 percent on the back of a 36.4 percent median jump in Prism Rare cards. Across all eight games, cards falling outnumbered risers 10,915 to 9,969. Price window: 2026-10-02 to 2026-10-09, ungraded market prices.

“Tracked” cards are those with a market price on both ends of the week and worth at least $5 at the start — cheaper cards are left out because a few cents of movement reads as a large, meaningless percentage. A group like “12 of 15 tracked” may belong to a bigger set; the rest simply couldn’t be measured honestly this week.

The drift chart will show most games bunched in mildly negative territory, with Gundam the clear outlier to the downside and Pokémon and Yu-Gi-Oh the only two closing positive, the latter by just 0.1 percent. The bigger moves sat below that average, though: several games saw their newest supplemental releases cool hard, with Yu-Gi-Oh's Beyond the Brave down 59.4 percent at the median, Magic's Commander: Reality Fracture down 52.3 percent, and Pokémon's two 30th Celebration sets down 28.5 and 21.9 percent. Running the other way, select card families spiked against the grain, led by Magic's Nicol Bolas, Dragon-God cards, up 55.1 percent at the median, and Pokémon's Prism Rare cards, up 36.4 percent. Breadth stayed slightly negative, with 10,915 cards falling against 9,969 rising out of 38,675 tracked, though average gains of 5.1 percent outpaced average losses of 4.8 percent.

Model report card

Every forecast the model publishes is archived, and once its target date passes it is graded against what the price actually did — misses included. Four numbers summarize the record:

  • Typical miss — the average gap between the predicted and realized price. A 5% typical miss on a $100 card means the model's calls landed about $5 from reality.
  • Bias — the direction of the average error. Above zero, forecasts ran high (the model was optimistic); below zero it undershot. Near zero is best.
  • 80% band — every forecast ships with a low–high range the model expects to contain the real price 80% of the time. This column is how often it actually did: 80% is perfect calibration, higher means the bands are cautious, lower means overconfident.
  • Direction — when the model called a move of at least 1% and the price also moved at least 1%, how often it picked the right side, up or down. 50% would be a coin flip; a call against a flat month is neither right nor wrong and isn't counted.
